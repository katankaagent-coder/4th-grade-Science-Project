# Teaching Style Guide (read before writing any lesson)

**Learner:** Kelenna, 9, 4th grade, Dickinson TX. Bright, but his attention drifts when talk gets long, dry or full of jargon. Our job: he *understands* (first principles), and he can *pass the school test* (TEKS words, STAAR 2.0 question styles).

## The 8 rules

1. **One idea per step.** A step is 60-120 seconds: 4-9 `say` beats, plus one thing to do (predict, task or check). If you need "and also", make a new step.
2. **Start from what he can touch.** Open each idea with something from a 9-year-old's life: bath toys, a bike, a trampoline, a pool noodle, a flashlight, his shadow at recess, a hurricane on the news, crawfish, shrimp boats, Galveston Bay, the Kemah boardwalk, NASA in Clear Lake.
3. **Climb the idea ladder.** Each rung builds on the last. Never use an idea before its rung. The science word arrives *after* the idea: "That push or pull has a name. Scientists call it a force."
4. **Short and spoken.** Max 14 words per spoken sentence (the build fails otherwise). Talk like a fun older cousin, not a textbook. Use "you". Ask questions. Use sound words (CLUNK, SPLASH) sparingly.
5. **Predict before you show.** Every lesson has at least 3 `predict` beats, each followed by a cue that shows the answer and then a `verdict`. Guessing wrong is celebrated ("Surprise! Scientists get surprised all the time").
6. **Hands on.** Each `play` step has a `task` that Kelenna must do in the 3D scene (drag, tap, slide). The scene emits the event when he succeeds.
7. **Name the mistake.** Every wrong answer's `why` names the *specific* wrong idea and fixes it in one or two short sentences. Never just "That is wrong." Example: "Heavy things do not always sink. A huge log floats! It is about how tightly packed the stuff is."
8. **Bridge to the test.** Vocab `test` uses school words. In the quiz, about half the questions use STAAR-style setups ("A student tested...", "Which TWO...", tables of data in words), but still with short sentences.

## Banned and preferred words
Never: ferrous, illuminated, cyclical, phenomenon, approximately, therefore, thus, utilize, significant, molecules, kinetic, velocity, buoyancy (the lint tool enforces a list).
Prefer: stuff (then "matter"), tiny pieces / particles, push or pull (then "force"), packed tight / spread out, about, so, use.
Numbers: round and kid-sized ("about 30 days", "about 100 degrees Fahrenheit"). Texas kids know Fahrenheit; give Celsius only where the TEKS needs it and say both.

## Accuracy guardrails (common 4th-grade misconceptions to fight, never reinforce)
- Heavy things do not always sink; floating depends on how packed the stuff is compared with water (relative density).
- Dissolved salt or sugar is still there; mass stays the same (conservation of matter).
- Gravity pulls things down even when they are sitting still; friction is a force between touching surfaces.
- Energy moves through a wave; the water mostly bobs up and down in place.
- A circuit must be a complete closed loop; a switch opens or closes the loop.
- Seasons come from Earth's tilt, NOT from being closer to or farther from the Sun.
- Moon phases come from how much of the sunlit half we see, NOT Earth's shadow.
- Weather is today; climate is the long-term pattern (30 years or more).
- Weathering breaks; erosion moves; deposition drops.
- Producers make food from sunlight; decomposers recycle; energy flows from the Sun.
- Inherited traits come from parents; learned behaviors are taught or practiced; acquired changes (a scar) are not passed on.
